# Week 11 Self-Reflection

Eugene Obunde Okumu | Team NULL_TERMINATORS

What went well. I opened with the ask, KES 5 million for a three-month, one-station pilot, so the Board knew the decision before the detail. I showed the arithmetic behind every figure (net savings of KES 25.1 million a year), which invites challenge. I stress-tested the case: at half the benefits, payback is still about 17 months. Being upfront that the 84% catch rate comes from simulated data, and tying the KES 10 million rollout to a clear gate (at least 80% of failures caught, no more than 5% false alarms), turned my biggest weakness into a control.

What needs improvement. Several inputs, including the 30-pump fleet, six failures a year and the repair costs, are planning assumptions, and a sharp director will probe them. I need KPC actuals and a rehearsed answer for why half of a Kiboko-size loss is preventable. My walkthrough was one narration with no live questions, so I have not yet practised handling interruptions or explaining, in plain words, how the system learns what "healthy" looks like. Next time I will rehearse the five hardest questions, keep the pitch to 13 minutes to leave room for discussion, and pause more for the Board to react.
